TeaLeaf Development Log
========================

This file tracks development progress on TeaLeaf, a Thief-style VR stealth game
(Unity, OpenXR + XR Interaction Toolkit). Newest updates are at the top.


2026-09-27
----------
- MovementState (from 2026-09-26) verified in headset - all states read
  correctly via MovementStateDebug.
- Ray-targeted climb grab, step 1 of 3 (Phase 1). A hand now grabs the
  ClimbableEdge its hand ray/reticle is on while grip is held, instead of
  whatever its SphereCollider overlapped - what's highlighted is exactly
  what gets grabbed. Chose "held" over "press only" to keep the old
  hold-grip-and-reach-in feel. PlayerHandInteraction now exposes
  LeftTargetPoint/RightTargetPoint (the ray hit point) alongside
  LeftTarget/RightTarget; PlayerClimbing takes a PlayerHandInteraction
  reference and checks "rayTarget is ClimbableEdge" (a cheap type check,
  no GetComponent). Removed the sphere fields, FindEdgeOverlapping(), and
  ClimbableEdge's Active list, Bounds, Overlaps() and ClosestPoint(), which
  only existed for the overlap check. The visual hand is pinned at the ray
  hit point for now; step 2 replaces that with a general hand snap pose.
  Grab reach: no separate distance - the 0.5m hand ray already is arm's
  reach. Removed the climb SphereColliders from Left/Right Hand and wired
  PlayerClimbing's new reference. Verified working in headset.
- Hand snap poses, step 2 of 3 (Phase 1). A general way for any grab
  target to say where a hand visual goes and how it's posed:
  HandPose (enum of finger poses), HandSnapPose (readonly struct: world
  position, rotation, pose) and IHandSnapTarget (GetSnapPose(isLeftHand,
  grabPoint)), all in Interaction. The target works out a model-agnostic
  "grip frame"; a shared HandSnapProfile ScriptableObject then applies
  per-hand position/rotation offsets (per hand because the right visual is
  mirrored). Chose a shared asset over per-edge fields so the offsets are
  tuned once for every ledge. ClimbableEdge implements it: the ray hit is
  moved to the box's top-front line in local space (clamped to the edge's
  length), facing into the wall. New convention: edge local X along the
  edge, +Y up, +Z out from the wall.
  HandVisualSnap (plain C# class, one per hand, owned by PlayerClimbing for
  now) blends the visual to the snap pose over 0.08s with SmoothStep and
  back on release, and skips touching the transform entirely at rest.
  Found while doing this: the old pinning ran inside PlayerClimbing.Tick(),
  before turning and Move() - since the visuals are children of the rig,
  every pinned hand was dragged off by that frame's movement until the next
  frame. Visuals are now placed in PlayerClimbing.TickHandVisuals(), a new
  last step (9) in PlayerController.Update(). Finger pose is carried in the
  snap pose but not played yet (step 3).
- Bug: snapping worked in headset, but a snapped hand still moved slightly
  with controller rotation/movement. Cause: the hands' Tracked Pose Drivers
  use "Update And Before Render" (m_UpdateType 0), so just before rendering
  - after all our Update() code - they move the controller again with
  fresher tracking data, dragging any child visual with it. Setting the
  pose later can't beat that reliably (before-render callback order isn't
  guaranteed). Fix: HandVisualSnap detaches the visual (SetParent(null,
  true)) on snap and re-attaches it to the controller when the release
  blend ends. Parented to the scene root rather than the ledge, because the
  ledges are non-uniformly scaled cubes and a rotated child under one would
  shear. Lesson: anything that must stay world-fixed can't be a child of a
  tracked transform. Verified in headset - snapped hands now stay dead
  still on the ledge. Step 2 done.


2026-09-26
----------
- Roadmap change: added a Phase 1 item (before Jump) for ray-targeted climb
  grabs. A hand will start climbing when grip is pressed while its hand
  ray/reticle is on a ClimbableEdge, instead of when its SphereCollider
  overlaps one - the climb spheres get removed. On grab the visual hand
  snaps to the edge with a defined position, rotation and a "fingers over
  the lip" pose. This snap is designed as a general concept (a hand snap
  pose that any grab target supplies, per hand) so ladders, ropes, doors,
  tools and props reuse it rather than each special-casing the hand.
  Updated the ladder/rope, hand pose, physical hands and grab pose items to
  match, and added an open decision on max grab reach (the ray is longer
  than an arm).
- MovementState (Phase 1): a single enum - Still, Walking, Sprinting,
  CrouchStill, CrouchWalking, Climbing, Airborne - on PlayerLocomotion, for
  noise/visibility/AI to read. Crouch is folded into the enum rather than
  kept as a separate flag, since crouched-still and crouched-moving need
  different noise/visibility anyway. Computed in a new TickState(), called
  by PlayerController right after Move(), because it needs the real applied
  movement (so sprinting into a wall reads as Still) and the fresh
  isGrounded. Airborne waits for a 0.15s grace period because isGrounded
  flickers on steps and slopes. Added MovementStateDebug (logs transitions)
  to verify it.
- Sprint (Phase 1). Click-to-toggle on the left stick click rather than
  hold-to-sprint, since holding a stick click while pushing that stick is
  tiring. sprintSpeed (4.5 m/s vs 3 walk) replaces moveSpeed while sprinting;
  the sprint ends when the stick returns to centre, on a second click, on
  crouch or on climb, and can't start while crouched. Exposed as
  PlayerLocomotion.IsSprinting. Verified working in headset.
- Input: found crouch was wired to XRI's "Jump" action (right A), a
  confusingly named action in the XRI sample asset. New gameplay actions now
  live in the project-wide Assets/InputSystem_Actions.inputactions (Unity 6
  creates and auto-enables it) instead: gave its Player/Sprint action an XR
  binding (left stick click - it had a placeholder "any controller trigger"
  binding) and added an XR binding for Player/Crouch (right A). Jump will use
  its Player/Jump action on right B instead of A. PlayerInputXR now enables
  every action it reads in OnEnable(), since they come from two assets
  enabled by two different things.
- Roadmap change: deferred the Phase 0 Quest 3 standalone baseline build to
  Phase 10. Development stays PCVR-first for now; Quest 3 standalone is still
  the eventual target, so the Quest-minded performance habits continue. That
  closes out Phase 0 - next up is Phase 1 (sprint first).
- Restructured the player in Main.unity: unpacked the XR Origin prefab,
  renamed PlayerRig to Player, deleted XRI's unused Locomotion object, and
  moved PlayerHandInteraction/PlayerHandAnimation onto a new Hands object
  (under Camera Offset) that now parents Left Hand/Right Hand. Body systems
  stay on the root, hand systems on Hands. Hands must stay under Camera
  Offset with an identity transform, or tracked hand poses and crouch would
  be offset. PlayerController verified working after the refactor;
  PlayerController.Reset() now finds the hand components in children.
- Added PlayerController (Phase 0 tick orchestrator). PlayerLocomotion.Update()
  had grown into two jobs - locomotion, and running every other player system
  in order - and Phase 1 was about to pile sprint, jump, mantling and ladders
  into it. PlayerController is now the only player script with an Update():
  it ticks input, body (centre + crouch), hand rays, climbing and hand
  animation in order, collects movement from PlayerLocomotion.TickMovement()
  and PlayerClimbing.FrameMovement into _frameMovement, calls Move() once,
  and reports the applied movement back to climbing. PlayerLocomotion lost
  its Update() and its references to the other systems; HandleMovement()/
  HandleGravity() now return their movement instead of writing to a shared
  field. Frame order and behaviour are unchanged. PlayerController.Reset()
  auto-fills its references when added to PlayerRig (Reset() is an
  editor-only callback, free at runtime).
- Design change: jumping is now in the project (it was previously decided
  against). Added a Jump item to Phase 1 ahead of climb release momentum,
  since both need the same horizontal air momentum in PlayerLocomotion -
  build it once for jump, then reuse it for pushing off ledges. Also added
  an Airborne movement state, landing noise (Phase 2 footsteps), jump
  cases in the test scene, and jumping to the comfort pass.
- Roadmap change: added climb release momentum to Phase 1. Letting go of a
  ledge currently drops the player with no momentum; the plan is to average
  the climb's actually-applied velocity over the last few frames and carry
  it into the fall on release (vertical into _verticalVelocity, horizontal
  into a new decaying air momentum), so pushing off a ledge launches you.
- Physics layers set up (Phase 0): Environment, Player, PlayerHands,
  Interactable, Climbable, Guard, GuardHead, Projectile (user layers 6-13),
  assigned in Main.unity. Dropped ShadowVolume and NoiseOccluder from the
  planned list - visibility will use a self-registered point-in-box check
  like ClimbableEdge (no physics), and walls on Environment can block sound.
  Collision matrix trimmed to the pairs that actually need contacts (see
  CLAUDE.md). Hand rays now only hit Environment/Interactable/Climbable, so
  walls block them and edges can no longer be highlighted through a wall.
  Learned: the collision matrix only governs contacts, triggers and
  CharacterController blocking - raycasts/overlaps ignore it and use their
  own LayerMask.
- Bug: NullReferenceException in HandRayReticle.Tick() when entering Play
  Mode before the controllers were tracked. XRI's XR Input Modality Manager
  on PlayerRig deactivates the Left/Right Controller objects in its
  OnEnable when no controller is connected yet - and PlayerRig is set up
  before its children, so the reticles (children of those objects) never
  got their Awake() and had no MeshRenderer. Not caused by the layer work,
  just newly noticed. Fixed by skipping Tick() while the reticle isn't
  active. Anything else living under the controller objects can hit the
  same thing.
- Roadmap change: added physical hands to the slice scope (Phase 3, before
  grabbing). The visual hand model will collide with the world and stop at
  walls instead of passing through, "elastic banding" back to the real
  controller position once clear (Half-Life: Alyx / Thief VR style). Needs
  dedicated primitive hand colliders; the collision approach (kinematic
  sweeps vs a velocity-driven Rigidbody) will be compared for Quest 3 cost
  before building it.
- Hand animation finished: both hands curl the index finger (trigger) and the
  middle/ring/pinky (grip) correctly in Play Mode. Learned two things:
  Avatar Masks turned out to be unnecessary, because with a Generic rig a
  layer only writes the properties its clips actually key, and each clip only
  keys its own finger bones. And the right hand is the same hand.fbx
  mirrored with scale.x -1 (its bones are still named J_Left_*), so one
  Animator Controller serves both hands. Worth remembering for physical
  hands: the right hand's colliders will sit under a negative scale.
- Scoped a greybox vertical slice: one docks/warehouse level where the player
  steals an objective item and escapes, with getting caught failing and
  restarting the level. Agreed mechanics: shadow-volume visibility (torches
  linked to the volumes they light, so water bolts darken areas), footstep/
  impact/door noise, patrol-suspicion-search-chase-catch guard AI (no
  combat), blackjack takedowns, an either-hand crossbow with water/
  noisemaker/rope bolts that's physically cocked, rotate-to-find-the-sweet-
  spot lockpicking, a wrist radial menu for tools, loot pocketing, sprint,
  mantling, ladders, rope climbing, and dragging KO'd bodies. Guards use a
  placeholder humanoid with Mixamo animations.
- Created DEVROADMAP.txt (next to this file) with the phased plan (Phase 0
  finish hand animation -> Phase 10 Quest 3 performance pass) and milestones
  M1-M6. It replaces the old "Next up" list below: sprint and ladders are now
  Phase 1, and the settings framework is out of scope for the slice.
- Brought CLAUDE.md up to date with the code as it actually is (crouch,
  PlayerClimbing, PlayerHandInteraction, HandRayReticle, PlayerHandAnimation,
  the Interaction namespace, the explicit Tick() frame order) plus the slice
  design, and added a rule to keep CLAUDE.md, DEVLOG.txt and DEVROADMAP.txt
  updated alongside every change.
- Fixed version control. The repo had no .gitignore and only tracked 43
  files: no ProjectSettings/, no Packages/, almost no .meta files (so a
  fresh clone would lose every script reference in Main.unity), and no
  art/XR assets. Added a Unity .gitignore (Library/Temp/Logs/obj/
  UserSettings, generated .csproj/.sln, .idea/) and committed the whole
  project properly. Also had to mark the Dropbox folder as a git
  safe.directory, since it's owned by Administrators.
- Left the Blender source files (Blends/hand.blend and its .blend1 backup,
  167 MB each) out of git. They're over GitHub's 100 MB file limit, and
  they're still backed up in Dropbox. *.blend is ignored until Git LFS is
  set up for them.


2026-09-13
----------
- Replaced the placeholder hand controller models with the real hand models,
  correctly oriented in the game world.
- Designed and started building a hand finger-animation system. Added a
  HandState enum (Idle vs Climbing) inside a new PlayerHandAnimation.cs -
  Idle drives finger curl from live grip/trigger input, Climbing (and any
  future interaction states) intentionally does nothing yet, since a gripped
  ledge should own the hand's pose instead once that's built.
- Added IsLeftHandGripping/IsRightHandGripping to PlayerClimbing.cs (reading
  the existing per-hand _leftGrabbedEdge/_rightGrabbedEdge fields directly),
  since the existing IsClimbing only reflects whichever hand is the primary
  movement-driving hand, not each hand's own grip state - PlayerHandAnimation
  needs the latter.
- Wired PlayerHandAnimation.Tick() into PlayerLocomotion.Update(), right
  after playerClimbing.Tick(), so it always reads this frame's grab state
  rather than last frame's.
- Settled on a per-hand Animator approach for finger curl: each hand's
  Animator Controller gets one masked layer per finger group (index; middle
  + ring + pinky), each a 1D blend tree between an Open and a Closed pose
  clip, driven by GripCurl (middle/ring/pinky) and TriggerCurl (index) float
  parameters read from PlayerInputXR. Thumb is left untouched for now.
- Confirmed the hand FBX's Animation Type is Generic (not Humanoid), and
  got the bone hierarchy for one hand's fingers (J_Left_Hand_Index1-4,
  mirrored per finger and per side).
- Learned the Animation window's per-bone workflow: Add Property (not the
  Add Keyframe diamond) is what actually creates a curve for a
  not-yet-animated bone, and pose clips used in blend trees need exactly ONE
  keyframe each (a static pose) rather than an open-to-closed sequence
  within a single clip - otherwise the Animator plays that clip's own
  internal timeline independently of the curl parameter.
- Built and verified the index finger end-to-end on both hands: recorded
  Hand_L/R_Index_Open and Hand_L/R_Index_Closed pose clips, added a
  TriggerCurl parameter and 1D blend tree to each hand's Animator
  Controller, and assigned both real Animators into PlayerHandAnimation's
  Left Hand Animator / Right Hand Animator slots. Confirmed in Play Mode
  that pulling either trigger smoothly curls that hand's index finger.
- Hit a "Parameter 'Hash ...' does not exist" console error once both hands
  were wired up - UpdateHand() always sets both GripCurl and TriggerCurl
  every frame, but neither controller had a GripCurl parameter defined yet
  (that layer isn't built). Fixed by adding an unused GripCurl float
  parameter to both controllers now, ready for when the middle/ring/pinky
  layer is added.

Still to do next: middle+ring+pinky Open/Closed pose clips (posed together,
per hand), an Avatar Mask per finger group per hand (index-only; middle +
ring + pinky-only) so each blend tree layer only touches its own bones, and
the second masked layer + blend tree on each hand's Animator Controller
before this feature is complete.

- Confirmed working so far: head/controller tracking, stick movement, smooth turn,

- Built a CharacterController test scene with a 0.2m step, a 30 degree ramp, and
  a 60 degree ramp. Initially could climb all three (including the 60 degree
  ramp, which shouldn't be climbable). Fixed by setting the CharacterController's
  Slope Limit to 45 degrees and Step Offset to 0.3m on the player rig in
  Main.unity - now correctly climbs the 0.2m step and 30 degree ramp but not the
  60 degree ramp (roadmap item 2 complete).
- Added a button-driven crouch system to PlayerLocomotion.cs/PlayerInputXR.cs:
  a new HandleCrouch() toggles _isCrouching on each press of a new CrouchPressed
  input, then smoothly moves the CharacterController's height between the
  existing minimumHeight field (now doing double duty as the crouched height)
  and the standing height captured from the Inspector at startup, keeping the
  controller's centre and a new cameraOffsetTransform in sync each frame so the
  player's view drops even though they haven't physically crouched.
- Wired up the crouch system (crouch button binding + Camera Offset transform
  assignment) and set Tracking Origin Mode to Device with Camera Y Offset 1.6
  on the XR Origin. Hit a bug: crouching dropped the view under the floor,
  because HandleCrouch() was overwriting the Camera Offset transform's Y
  position outright instead of adjusting it, discarding the XR Origin's 1.6
  baseline. Fixed by making the crouch code apply a relative delta (however
  much the CharacterController's height changed that frame) instead of an
  absolute value, so it nudges whatever offset is already there rather than
  replacing it. Confirmed working correctly in-headset (roadmap item 3
  complete).
