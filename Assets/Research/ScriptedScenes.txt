SCRIPTED SCENES - CHARACTERS MOVING AND SPEAKING TO A SCRIPT
===========================================================

How to make in-level scripted moments: a character walking a set way,
saying lines at set points, a conversation that ends and hands over to
normal AI. Part 1 is how Thief VR does it (its prologue's two guards at
the market alley brazier). Part 2 is the options for TeaLeaf, with costs
and a recommendation.

Source for part 1: the extracted Thief VR prologue
(E:\ThiefVR_Export\Prologue\ExportedProject, gameplay scene). The game is
IL2CPP, so scripts are empty stubs: the serialized fields and UnityEvent
wiring are real, what the code does with them is inferred and marked so.


PART 1 - THIEF VR: THE TWO GUARDS AT THE BRAZIER
================================================

WHAT THE PLAYER SEES
--------------------

Two guards stand by the brazier in the market alley. As the player comes
near they talk (27 lines, a zealot and a moaner arguing about the Baron),
then both walk off on patrol routes they keep for the rest of the level.
If the player is spotted mid-conversation, they still end up on the same
patrols.


NO TIMELINE, NO CUTSCENE TOOL
-----------------------------

The scene uses no Timeline. The whole thing is five plain pieces, each a
component wired to the next by UnityEvents in the Inspector:

  1. trigger volumes the player walks into
  2. a delay component
  3. guard objects switched on and off
  4. one "eavesdropping situation" component holding the conversation
  5. the guards' normal patrol system, given a new route at the end

The scene's only PlayableDirectors (Timeline) are two in
"Cutscene - PrologueTutorial", the intro cutscene: own guard rig, own
characters, a looping idle timeline chained to the main one
(TimelinePlayer: looping, nextTimelinePlayer, completeEvent,
sceneTransitionOnComplete). Timeline is kept for the non-interactive
cutscene; everything that happens in the playable level is triggers plus
AI.


THE CHAIN, IN ORDER
-------------------

Positions are world metres in the prologue (brazier at 23.2, -1, -38.2).

a) Level start: two stand-in guards at the fire
   = NPC/SG_GuardPatrol_PlayerAtBalcony01 and ...02, a metre either side
   of the brazier. Full guards (senses, awareness, ragdoll), each with a
   one-point "WaypointsIDLE" route: they just stand there. These cover the
   view from the starting house's balcony.

b) BalconyGuardSwap (box trigger 2.5 x 2.9 x 1.8m on the balcony,
   player body only). On enter, UnityEvents:
     - PlayerAtBalcony01 guard   SetActive(false)
     - PlayerAtBalcony02 guard   SetActive(false)
     - Pre-StealthHit guard      SetActive(true)   ("STARTS OFF")
     - PreObj guard              SetActive(true)   ("STARTS OFF")
     - the trigger itself        SetActive(false)  (fires once)
   The two new guards stand in exactly the same spots. So the guards who
   will talk are swapped in, fresh, out of the player's sight.
   Inferred why: a guard placed with its scene state ready is simpler than
   resetting one that may have been alerted from the balcony. The swap is
   a cheap "reset the actors" step.

c) SetMarketGuardsToIdle (box 5.2 x 3.5 x 0.5m, player body or head).
   On enter: EnterIdleBehaviour() on both new guards - pins them standing
   at their idle point (each has a one-point WaypointsIDLE route too).

d) ActivateMarketEavesdrop (box 2.1 x 3.5 x 0.5m, ~12m from the fire,
   player body or head). On enter: DelayedEvent.TriggerDelayedEvent(),
   which after delayTime 2.5s does pfb_Eavesdropping.SetActive(true).
   The conversation starts when its object switches on.

e) EavesdroppingSituation (on pfb_Eavesdropping, at the fire):
     eavesdropBlockName   FirstEavesdrop
     speakerMappings      name -> guard  (Pre-StealthHit_Helmetless,
                                          PreObj_Helmetless)
     timeline             27 entries, each:
                            Speaker_RF  which guard speaks
                            EventKey    audio event, e.g.
                                        ZEALOT/CH00_EAVESDROP_MAIN_LN001
                            EventLine   subtitle text
                            Buffer      0.2s gap after the line
     eavesRadius          12.36m
     checkType            0
     raycastCheckMask     Default + layer 20 (Door)
     disableAnimation     0
     onCompletion         SetPatrolRoute(WaypointsPATROL) on each guard
     onFail               the same two calls
   Plus EavesdroppingSaveable and GameObjectActiveSaveable (save games
   remember whether it ran).

   Inferred:
   - Lines play one after another: line's audio length + Buffer, then the
     next. The "timeline" is just an ordered list, not Unity Timeline.
   - Speakers are guards, so lines come from their heads; animation runs
     unless disableAnimation (talking gestures).
   - eavesRadius + raycast mask: the player must be within 12.36m and not
     behind a wall or door for it to start or carry on, so the player
     doesn't miss it. (Exact rule unknown: may pause between lines, may
     only gate the start.)
   - onFail: a speaker leaves its idle/talk behaviour (sees the player,
     hears a noise, is knocked out). Conversation stops; the survivors
     get the same patrols as if it had ended. Both endings converge, so
     the level beyond doesn't care how the scene went.

f) Afterwards: ordinary AI on ordinary routes.
   PreStealthHit WaypointsPATROL: 8 points, cyclical.
   PreObj WaypointsPATROL: 2 points, cyclical.
   Each PatrolPoint:
     type             0 = walk through, 1 = stop here
     IdleTime         seconds stopped (5 on the stop points)
     AnimationIndex   which idle animation to play while stopped
     barkOptions      lines to say there (empty everywhere in the prologue)
   So "say a line at a point on a route" is a route point property, no
   scene object needed.

g) Later swap on the objective. Picking up the Keeper Relic (DoOnceEvent
   on it) fires, among tutorial prompts:
     - MedallionPickup15 narration
     - PreObj guard (at the fire) SetActive(false)
     - PostObj guard (same model, other side of the alley) SetActive(true)
   The level changes one guard's patrol after the objective by swapping
   in another pre-placed guard, not by re-routing.

Pattern: a small library of single-purpose event components chained by
UnityEvents in the Inspector, with designer-facing names on every object
("STARTS OFF", "BalconyGuardSwap"). No visual scripting graph for level
events (the guards' own brains are a behaviour graph, separate).


LESSONS
-------

- Scripted moments in a playable stealth level are AI with a script laid
  over it, not cutscenes. The actors stay real guards throughout: they
  can see, hear and be knocked out mid-line.
- Every scene has an interrupted ending, and it hands over to the same
  state as the finished one.
- Movement is navigation to points, never keyframed: the guard's feet,
  animation and avoidance keep working.
- Lines are data (speaker, audio, text, gap), played in order.
- Start the scene where the player will hear it: trigger placed on the
  approach, a short delay, a range check.
- Swapping in pre-placed actors is their cheap way to reset or re-route.


PART 2 - OPTIONS FOR TEALEAF
============================

What TeaLeaf has today (Assets/Scripts/AI/CLAUDE.md): Guard state machine
(Patrol / Suspicious / Search / Chase / Stunned / KnockedOut), PatrolRoute
(child points, one waitTime for all, loops or ping-pong), barks as
SoundCues played from the head by SoundPlayer (room/portal routed,
SpatialVoice, sound only - other guards don't react). Not built: chatter,
patrol idle variety (clips scratch, fold_arms, wait unused). Unity
Timeline is not in Packages/manifest.json.

Three scales of solution.


OPTION A - UNITY TIMELINE
-------------------------

A PlayableDirector per scene: animation tracks for guards, audio tracks
for lines, signals for "now go patrol".

  Build:    package add; custom track for lines (Timeline's audio track
            bypasses SoundPlayer, so no rooms/portals/SpatialVoice, no
            muffling through doors); custom track or signals to drive
            guards; code to stop the director when a guard is alerted.
  Maintain: two systems moving guards (Timeline and Guard) that must not
            fight: an animation track overrides the Animator, position
            keys ignore the NavMesh, GuardFootLock and GuardAnimation
            assume agent movement.
  Runtime:  a PlayableGraph per director while playing; modest.
  Gives:    visual editing, scrubbing, exact timing.

Fits a non-interactive cutscene (Thief VR's intro). Wrong tool for a
guard who may spot the player mid-sentence: interruption is the normal
case in a stealth level, and Timeline is built for runs that finish.
Cutscenes are out of scope for the slice.


OPTION B - ROUTE POINTS THAT DO THINGS (single guard, no scene object)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Extend PatrolRoute: each point can hold its own wait, an idle animation,
a facing, and a line to say on arrival (Thief's PatrolPoint fields:
type, IdleTime, AnimationIndex, barkOptions). Plus Guard.SetPatrolRoute()
so an event can give a guard a new route.

  Build:    small - a PatrolPoint component (optional per child; a child
            without one keeps the route's waitTime), the guard reads it on
            arrival; a few Animator states for the idle clips.
  Maintain: low; it is the patrol system, not a new one. Alerted guards
            already leave Patrol and come back to the nearest point.
  Runtime:  nothing new per frame.
  Gives:    "walk this way, stop here, scratch, say this, carry on" for
            one guard. Not a conversation (no taking turns between two
            speakers, no "start when the player arrives").


OPTION C - A SCENE COMPONENT (Thief's eavesdrop, generalised a little)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

One component per scripted moment, e.g. AI.GuardScene:
  - actors: the guards taking part
  - steps, in order, each one of:
      Say    speaker, SoundCue, subtitle text (kept for later), gap after
      GoTo   actor, point transform (NavMesh walk, Guard's own GoTo)
      Face   actor, point or other actor
      Wait   seconds
      (Animate: actor, idle clip - once the Animator has the states)
  - when done: each actor's route afterwards (SetPatrolRoute)
  - if broken off: the same, by default (Thief's onFail = onCompletion)
  - started by a trigger, or on level start; optional delay

Guard side: while in a scene, a guard stays in Patrol but the scene drives
it instead of the route (a "held by scene" flag; the scene calls GoTo,
FaceTowards, Bark-like Say). Senses stay on. Any state change out of
Patrol (suspicious, chase, stunned, KO) ends the scene for everyone:
remaining actors get their after-routes (a speaker alone mid-argument
shouldn't stand talking to nobody). Lines are sound only, no noise event,
as chatter (root CLAUDE.md).

Trigger: a box on PlayerBlocker (collides only with Player, in no query
mask, so nothing else trips or sees it) with a small ScriptTrigger
component: on player enter, once, after an optional delay, start the
scene (or fire a UnityEvent, for the "swap a guard after the objective"
kind of thing). OnTriggerEnter from the CharacterController; the
controller is off during a mantle, so the trigger fires on the first move
after one.

  Build:    medium - GuardScene (steps list + a tiny state machine in its
            own Update while running, idle otherwise), ScriptTrigger, a
            few public hooks on Guard (SetPatrolRoute, enter/leave held,
            Say returning the line's length, an event when it leaves
            Patrol). SoundPlayer.Play returns only a bool: Say needs the
            chosen clip's length back (or the SoundCue exposes it) to time
            the next line. Dialogue cues: one clip each, pitchVariation 0
            (pitch changes the length).
  Maintain: one place for all scripted moments; steps visible in the
            Inspector in order; guards stay the only thing moving guards.
  Runtime:  nothing while idle; while running, one Update with a timer
            check. No allocations (steps are serialized data). Lines go
            through SoundPlayer, so they are heard through portals, muffled
            by shut doors, placed by SpatialVoice - eavesdropping through a
            doorway works and sounds true.
  Gives:    the brazier scene and most in-level scripted moments: two or
            more guards talking, a guard walking somewhere to meet
            another, then everyone off on patrol.

Range check (Thief's eavesRadius 12.36m + ray): leave it out at first.
Place the trigger on the approach so the player is in earshot when it
starts; add "wait between lines while the player is out of earshot" only
if headset testing shows players missing it.


RECOMMENDATION
--------------

C, with B's SetPatrolRoute and per-point waits/lines as the first slice
of it if a single guard's moment is wanted sooner. Not Timeline.

Why: it matches what the shipped game does for exactly this kind of
scene; actors stay real guards, so interruption is the normal case and
needs no extra machinery; movement stays on the NavMesh with the foot
lock and animation intact; lines use the existing sound routing (the
project's "sound tells the truth" rule); nothing runs when no scene is
playing. Timeline only earns a place if a real cutscene is ever wanted.

Build order, smallest useful first:
  1. Guard.SetPatrolRoute(); ScriptTrigger (box on PlayerBlocker, once,
     delay, UnityEvent).
  2. GuardScene with Say and Wait only: two guards stand at a fire and
     talk, then get their routes. (Covers the brazier example.)
  3. GoTo / Face steps.
  4. Idle animations (Animator states for scratch, fold_arms, wait) for
     scene steps and patrol points.
  5. Subtitles, if ever (the text field is already in the data).

Voice-line pools that never repeat (root CLAUDE.md) are for barks and
idle chatter; a scene's lines are written once and play in order, so
they sit outside those pools.


AUTHORING A SCENE (as it would work)
------------------------------------

  1. Place the guards (prefab instances) where the scene starts; no route
     (they hold their placed pose) or a one-point route.
  2. Make each guard's after-scene PatrolRoute.
  3. Add a GuardScene object near them: actors, steps (speaker + cue +
     gap per line), after-routes.
  4. Add a ScriptTrigger box on the approach, on PlayerBlocker, pointing
     at the scene; set the delay.
  5. Test: walk in, listen; then walk in loud and get spotted mid-line -
     both guards must leave the scene and patrol/search as normal.

A level restart reloads the scene, so every scripted moment replays from
the start with nothing to reset.
