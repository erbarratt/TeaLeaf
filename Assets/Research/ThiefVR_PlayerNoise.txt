THIEF VR - PLAYER NOISE AND HEARING DISTANCES
=============================================

Source: the extracted Thief VR prologue (E:\ThiefVR_Export\Prologue\ExportedProject).
The game is IL2CPP, so the export has no code: every script is an empty stub.
Everything here comes from serialized data - NoiseLevelData / NoiseLevelManager
assets, AudioObjectProfile and SoundEventProfile assets, components on the
player rig (MT_XRRig_Quest) and in the prologue gameplay scene. The numbers are
real; how they combine at runtime is inferred and marked so where unsure.


NOISE LEVELS
------------

NoiseLevelManager holds five NoiseLevelData assets, quietest first:

  Level      Value  Radius   Path distance  Step particle
  Silent     0      0m       0m             no
  Low        1      2m       2.5m           no
  Medium     2      5m       6.25m          no
  High       3      10m      12.5m          yes
  VeryHigh   4      15m      18.75m         yes

- Path distance is always 1.25 x radius: a fixed allowance for sound
  going round corners.
- High and above shows a visible particle at each footstep, so the player
  sees when they are loud.


HOW A NOISE REACHES A GUARD
---------------------------

AISoundEventManager_RF (gameplay scene):
  debugNoiseData            High
  npcSelectionStrategy      0
  lineOfSightMask           Default layer
  rayCastDownCollisionMask  Default layer
  pathEndToSoundDistance    0.5m

NavMeshAudioSurface: positionTolerance 0.45m.

Inferred:
1. The noise is snapped down to the floor (ray down).
2. Guards inside the noise's radius are candidates.
3. A guard with clear line of sight to the noise hears it within the radius.
4. Otherwise a NavMesh path is walked from guard to noise; the guard hears it
   only if the path is under the path distance and ends within 0.5m of the
   noise.

So guard hearing follows walkable routes, never through walls. No rooms or
portals in guard hearing: AudioRoom / AudioPortal exist in the scene but seem
to serve the player's own audio (reverb, occlusion), not guards.


FOOTSTEPS
---------

PlayerFootstepEmitter (on the rig):
  maxStealthWalkVelocity  1.9 m/s
  defaultAudioProfile     Floor Template (Medium)

Step loudness comes from the floor's AudioObjectProfile. surfaceLoudness is an
index into the five levels; absorption appears to shift it (inferred):

  Floor                                         surfaceLoudness  absorption
  Carpet                                        1 (Low)          -2
  Dirt, softwood                                2 (Medium)       -1
  Stone, brick, hardwood, tile, roof tile,
    hard metal                                  2 (Medium)        0
  Sheet metal, hollow metal                     2 (Medium)       +1
  Glass, loose wood                             3 (High)          0

Rig speeds:
  Walk         2.4 m/s
  Run          3.8 m/s (run from 2.4)
  Crouch       1.4 m/s
  Fast crouch  1.8 m/s (from 1.3)

Every crouch speed is under the 1.9 m/s stealth limit; walking and running are
over it. No crouch multiplier for noise was found (the only *_CrouchMultiplier
fields are vision: suspicion and detection). Inferred: stance counts for noise
only through speed - under 1.9 m/s a step is quieter, so crouching is quiet
because it is slow, and a slow walk is quiet too.

No floor reaches VeryHigh on its own; whether running or metal absorption
pushes a step there can't be seen. VeryHigh is mostly used by loud props.


OTHER NOISES FROM THE PLAYER
----------------------------

Thrown / dropped props: each has its own AudioObjectProfile with a loudness
level, a size modifier and magnitudeThreshold (mostly 2.5) - an impact below
that makes no guard noise.

  Prop                                     Loudness
  Armour, glass                            4 (VeryHigh, 15m)
  Coins, mallet, ornate box, pouch         3 (High, 10m)
  Books, goblets, flasks                   2 (Medium, 5m)
  Paper, ball, dice                        1 (Low, 2m)

Distraction: armour, glass, mallet and Object_Silent have useDistractionEvent,
a separate guard event. Object_Silent has loudness 0 but still sends it, so a
prop can distract a guard without making noise.

Rattling held items: two "Shakee" objects on the rig (ShakeReciever) send the
BottleRattle event, Low (2m) - shaking a held item is heard close by.
(Vase, the other SoundEventProfile, is High.)


NOT VISIBLE IN THE EXPORT
-------------------------

- The loudness logic itself (how absorption, speed, size modifier combine).
- FMOD event audible ranges (in the FMOD banks, not the Unity data).


COMPARED WITH TEALEAF
=====================

TeaLeaf side read from Core/NoiseSystem.cs, Core/SoundPropagation.cs,
Core/SoundPortal.cs, Core/SoundCue.cs, Core/SurfaceSounds.cs,
Player/PlayerFootsteps.cs, Interaction/ImpactNoise.cs, AI/Guard.cs, and values
from Prefabs/Core/Player.prefab, Prefabs/Actors/Guard.prefab and
Data/Sound/Shared/Footsteps.


SIDE BY SIDE
------------

Loudness scale
  Thief VR: five fixed levels (0, 2, 5, 10, 15m). Everything picks one.
  TeaLeaf:  a free radius in metres on each SoundCue, multiplied by scales
    (gait, speed, impact hardness, surface). Any value is possible.

Route
  Thief VR: line of sight within the radius, else a NavMesh walking path up
    to 1.25 x radius, ending within 0.5m of the noise.
  TeaLeaf:  straight line in the same room; between rooms the shortest route
    through SoundPortals, which must fit inside the radius itself (no 1.25x
    allowance). A closed door adds 4m to the route (closedExtraDistance).
    A quick straight-line test rejects far listeners first.

Where the guard turns
  Thief VR: not visible in the data.
  TeaLeaf:  the noise itself in the same room, else the last portal on the
    route.

What the guard gets
  Thief VR: not visible (in or out of range at least; how strength is used
    is in code).
  TeaLeaf:  loudness = 1 - route length / radius (1 at the noise, 0 at the
    edge). Detection += loudness x 0.7 (hearingGain); suspicion += that x 3.33
    (hearingSuspicionScale), so suspicion fills from one noise heard within
    the nearer ~57% of its radius. A Distraction noise uses gain 1.5 instead.
    Search moves to a new noise once detection >= 0.5. Guards ignore guard
    noises.

Floor
  Thief VR: per-surface level index plus an absorption shift. Most floors
    (stone, wood, tile, brick, metal) Medium = 5m; carpet Low = 2m; glass and
    loose wood High = 10m.
  TeaLeaf:  per-surface footstep cue radius:
      Carpet 2.5m   Grass 3m   Wood 7m   Water 8m   Tile 9m   Gravel 9m
      Metal 10m     Stone 6m
    Surface loudness for props = surface radius / stone radius.

Stance and speed
  Thief VR: one speed limit, 1.9 m/s ("stealth walk"); all crouching is
    under it, walking (2.4) and running (3.8) over it. No crouch noise
    multiplier found.
  TeaLeaf:  gait scale walk 1, sprint 1.6, crouch 0.4; then speed scale
    between 0.4 (slowest creep) and 1 (full stick); a crouched step slower
    than 30% of full speed is silent. Walk 3 m/s, sprint 4.5 m/s, crouch at
    walking speed.

Landing
  Thief VR: no separate landing noise found.
  TeaLeaf:  surface step radius x 1.5 x fall strength (0.5-2, fall speed / 3).

Props
  Thief VR: a level per prop (armour/glass VeryHigh down to paper Low),
    silent below an impact threshold (~2.5); distraction is a separate event.
  TeaLeaf:  one cue radius per prop (ImpactPlaceholder 10m), scaled 0.3-1 by
    impact speed (1-8 m/s, silent below 1) and by the surface hit.
    Distraction is a NoiseType with its own gain.

Other player noises
  Thief VR: rattling held items (Low).
  TeaLeaf:  doors, bolts, chests, locks, pack storing, blackjack hit,
    putting out flames - each its cue's radius (creak 5m).


WORKED DISTANCES (stone, straight line, same room)
--------------------------------------------------

                     Thief VR        TeaLeaf (stone 6m)
  Walk, full speed   5m (6.25 path)  6m
  Walk, creeping     5m*             2.4m
  Sprint             5m+*            9.6m
  Crouch, full       5m*             2.4m
  Crouch, slowest    5m*             1.4m, then silent
  * Thief VR's exact speed effect isn't visible; under 1.9 m/s is quieter
    by some amount.


WHAT STANDS OUT
---------------

- Stone, TeaLeaf's reference surface, is 6m against Thief VR's 5m: close.
  Prop impact loudness is each surface's radius over stone's, so changing
  stone's radius changes every surface's impact loudness too.
- TeaLeaf's routes through rooms and portals are stricter than Thief VR's
  NavMesh allowance: a noise round a corner reaches no further than its
  radius. Thief VR's 1.25x could be matched with a radius a little larger.
- TeaLeaf grades loudness by distance and Thief VR (as far as the data
  shows) uses a handful of fixed steps; TeaLeaf's is finer, Thief VR's is
  easier for a player to learn.
- Thief VR's floors are mostly the same level (Medium); TeaLeaf's spread
  wider (2.5-10m besides stone), so the floor matters more.
