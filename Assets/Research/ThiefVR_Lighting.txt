THIEF VR - PROLOGUE LIGHTING, BRIGHTNESS AND GAMMA
=================================================

Source: the extracted Thief VR prologue (E:\ThiefVR_Export\Prologue\ExportedProject),
the game's real ProjectSettings (E:\ThiefVR_Export\Settings\ExportedProject) and
the IL2CPP metadata in the installed game (Shadow_Data\il2cpp_data\Metadata\
global-metadata.dat), which holds the names of every class, method and field.
The game is IL2CPP, so the export has no code: every script is an empty stub and
every shader is a stub with only its Properties block. Everything here comes from
serialized data (scenes, prefabs, materials, volume profiles, Bakery components)
plus those names. The numbers are real; how they combine at runtime is inferred
and marked "(inferred)" where unsure.

Scenes: the level is four additive scenes. Prologue_Mission_Primary_EnvironmentScene
holds all level art and nearly all lighting; _GameplayScene holds the
extinguishable fires, guards, interactables and the settings menu; _AudioScene
and _DebugScene hold no lights. The player rig is the prefab
Content\Common\XRRigs\MT_XRRig_Quest (used on every platform despite the name).


SUMMARY
-------

- Lighting is almost entirely baked with Bakery (the third-party GPU
  lightmapper), not Unity's lightmapper. 310 Bakery point/spot lights, a
  Bakery directional moon and a Bakery sky light bake into four 4096x4096
  lightmaps with shadowmasks. Every Unity Light component in the environment
  scene is disabled: they are authoring stand-ins only.
- Moving things (guards, hands, props) are lit by one Bakery Volume (a 3D
  texture of baked light over the whole level), not by light probes. There
  are no LightProbeGroups anywhere.
- The only runtime lights are six fires in the gameplay scene (candle groups,
  a fireplace, two braziers): Mixed point lights, warm orange, short range,
  flickering, extinguishable. Their realtime shadows are off unless a
  higher-quality setting turns them on.
- BRIGHTNESS and GAMMA in the options menu are two sliders driving one global
  URP post-processing Volume on the player camera: brightness = Color
  Adjustments "Post Exposure", gamma = the Lift Gamma Gain "Gamma" trackball's
  luminance (w). Both default to 0 (no change). Details below.
- Separately from that player setting, an art-directed colour grade
  (GlobalColorGradingController) pushes shadows towards blue and lifts them,
  through global shader values with up to three spherical zones (inferred: read
  in the level shaders, not a post pass).
- Atmosphere: exponential fog, dark blue-violet, density 0.015; a masked,
  two-layer cubemap skybox with flowing clouds tinted dark purple; five baked
  reflection probes; fake god-ray meshes and corona flare billboards on lamps.
  No real volumetrics, no bloom, no tonemapping override found.


1. BRIGHTNESS AND GAMMA OPTION
------------------------------

The settings UI
  MazeTheory.Shadow.GraphicsOptions (in the gameplay scene, on the options
  menu canvas; two pages). Serialized fields:
    page1, page2
    brightnessSlider, brightnessDisplay      (slider + text readout)
    gammaSlider, gammaDisplay
    aliasingButton, shadowDistanceButton, shadowQualityButton,
    lodCrossFadeButton, npcQualityButton, renderScaleButton,
    cullingDistanceButton, particleQualityButton

  Brightness slider (UnityEngine.UI.Slider):
    min -5, max +5, whole numbers OFF (continuous), default value 0
    OnValueChanged -> GraphicsOptions.ChangeBrightness
  Gamma slider:
    min -1, max +1, whole numbers OFF, default value 0
    OnValueChanged -> GraphicsOptions.ChangeGamma

  Method and field names from the metadata (no bodies survive):
    GetData, GetVolumeProfile, GetColorAdj, GetLiftGammaGain,
    CheckAndSetBrightness, CheckAndSetGamma        (apply saved value at load)
    UpdateBrightnessSlider, UpdateGammaSlider      (sync UI with saved value)
    ChangeBrightness, ChangeGamma                  (slider callbacks)
    ResetToDefaults
    private fields _vp (VolumeProfile), _colorAdj (ColorAdjustments),
    _liftGammaGain (LiftGammaGain)
  So: on load it finds a VolumeProfile, caches its ColorAdjustments and
  LiftGammaGain overrides, and the sliders write straight into them. The
  values are saved with the other options (GetData; where they are stored is
  not visible).

What the sliders drive
  A global URP Volume on the player rig:
    MT_XRRig_Quest/PlayerController/CameraRig/FloorOffset/CameraScale/
      Camera/PlayerVFXHemisphere
    Volume: Is Global on, priority 0, blend distance 0, weight 1
    Profile: PC_Brightness, two overrides:
      ColorAdjustments: active, ONLY postExposure overridden, value 0
                        (contrast, colour filter, hue shift, saturation not
                        overridden)
      LiftGammaGain:    active, ONLY gamma overridden, value (1,1,1,0)
                        (lift and gain not overridden)

  Brightness -5..+5 -> ColorAdjustments.postExposure (inferred to be the slider
  value as is). Post Exposure is in EV (photographic stops), applied in linear
  HDR before grading: each +1 doubles every pixel's value, -1 halves it.
  +5 = x32, -5 = x1/32; blacks stay black, everything else scales.

  Gamma -1..+1 -> LiftGammaGain.gamma.w (inferred: the range is exactly URP's
  range for the trackball's luminance offset). The colour stays (1,1,1); only
  the w offset moves. URP's gamma bends the midtones while leaving black and
  white points where they are: positive w brightens the darks and mids
  (lifts shadow detail without washing out bright lamps), negative darkens
  them.

  So the pair is the usual "brightness + gamma" split: brightness scales the
  whole image linearly (dark scenes get brighter but lamps clip sooner),
  gamma reshapes the curve so dark areas become readable without blowing
  out the lights. For a dark stealth game, gamma is the one that rescues a
  dim headset; brightness is the cruder one.

Requirements and costs
  - Needs post-processing on the camera. The rig's main camera has URP
    "Render Post Processing" ON, HDR ON, MSAA allowed, dithering OFF, stop-NaN
    OFF, no camera anti-aliasing (MSAA from quality instead). Any active
    override means URP runs its uber post pass with a colour grading LUT: a
    full-screen pass every frame, both eyes.
  - PostProcessController (on the rig; field "cameras" -> the main camera)
    has SetPostProcessStateBasedOnLevel, SetPostProcessState and
    usePostProcessing: post can be switched on/off per level. Where it is
    off, the brightness and gamma sliders would do nothing (inferred).

Other volume profiles found (not the player setting)
  Prologue_Global_Profile_PSVR2 - on "Environment/High Level Volume/Global
    Volume" in the environment scene (global, priority 0, weight 1). Both its
    overrides are switched OFF (active 0), so it does nothing as shipped on
    PC. What it would do (a console look kept ready, inferred):
      ColorAdjustments: contrast +15 (postExposure 0.1 present but not
                        overridden)
      LiftGammaGain:    lift w +0.059 (not overridden), gamma w -0.117,
                        gain w +0.191
    i.e. more contrast, darker mids, brighter highlights.
  PauseHandler Profile - on Level_Core/PauseHandler/Volume (gameplay scene):
    ColorAdjustments saturation -100: the world turns grey while paused.
  GhostCamDoF - a depth of field profile for the ghost/recording camera.
  No profile anywhere has Tonemapping, Bloom, Vignette, White Balance,
  Shadows Midtones Highlights or Channel Mixer overrides. Unless the URP
  asset (not in the export) sets tonemapping, HDR values above 1 simply clip
  (inferred).


2. ART-DIRECTED COLOUR GRADE (NOT A PLAYER SETTING)
---------------------------------------------------

MazeTheory.Shadow.GlobalColorGradingController, on the same
PlayerVFXHemisphere object as the brightness Volume:
  ColorGradingEnabled 1, ColorGradingIntensity 1
  defaultPreset:
    ShadowsIntensity     1.8
    MidtonesIntensity    0.35
    HighlightsIntensity  0
    ShadowsColor         (0, 0.231, 1)   strong blue
    MidtonesColor        white
    HighlightsColor      white
    ShadowsTintIntensity 0.2
    Midtones/HighlightsTintIntensity 0
    UseZoneGrading 1, FalloffBias 1
  Zones: three slots (Center, Radius, Intensity, LerpedIntensity), all zero in
  the prefab (filled at runtime), lerpSpeed 3.5.
  Methods: ApplyDefaultPreset, ApplyNewPreset, UpdateGradingState,
  UpdateGradingIntensity, UpdateTintsAndBrightness, UpdateZoneData, SetZone,
  SetZoneData, GetNextAvailableZoneIndex; cached shader IDs
  ColorGradingEnabledID, ColorGradingIntensityID, ShadowsIntensityID,
  MidtonesIntensityID, HighlightsIntensityID, ShadowsColorID, MidtonesColorID,
  HighlightsColorID, Shadows/Midtones/HighlightsTintIntensityID, UseZoneID,
  ZoneCountID, FalloffBiasID, TintCenter0-2ID ...

  Reading: it sets global shader values (Shader.SetGlobal...), so the grade is
  applied inside the level's own shaders, not as a full-screen pass (inferred;
  the view-hemisphere shader has only vignette and damage properties, and no
  volume profile does this). Shadows get boosted 1.8x and tinted 20% towards
  blue; midtones get a mild lift; highlights untouched. This is how the game
  keeps its darks readable and moonlit-blue without making lamps brighter.
  Zones let a few spheres (up to three) blend in a different grade locally,
  lerped as the player moves.

PlayerVFXController (same object): a view vignette (radius 0.8 normal, down
to 0.5, fade in 1s, out 0.5s), "lightDucking" (buffer 0.75, lerp 0.2) driven
by the player's light state (_trackedPlayerLightVisState), speed lines and a
damage overlay, all drawn by a hemisphere mesh around the head (shader
Unlit/PlayerViewVFXHemisphere), not post. Inferred: the vignette closes in
when the player is hidden in shadow, as the "you are hidden" cue.


3. RENDER PIPELINE AND PROJECT SETTINGS
---------------------------------------

  Unity 6000.1, URP, Linear colour space, lights use linear intensity and
  colour temperature (GraphicsSettings). The URP asset itself is not in the
  export (its reference is a placeholder), so its HDR precision, shadow
  cascades, additional-light limits and grading mode are unknown.

  One quality level, "Steam":
    shadows soft (2), shadow resolution Medium (1), shadow distance 40m
    MSAA 4x
    anisotropic filtering forced on (2)
    realtime reflection probes on (none are realtime in the prologue)
    vSync every frame
    LOD bias 3.8 (high: keeps detailed LODs far out), maximum LOD level 0
    pixel light count 2 (built-in setting; URP uses its own per-object limit)
  Player-facing options (GraphicsOptions): brightness, gamma, aliasing,
  shadow distance, shadow quality, LOD cross-fade, NPC quality, render
  scale, culling distance, particle quality.

  Main camera: clear to skybox, background black, near 0.01m, far 2000m,
  HDR on, MSAA allowed, occlusion culling on, dynamic resolution allowed.
  Depth and opaque textures "use pipeline setting" (unknown).


4. MAIN LIGHT - THE MOON
------------------------

  Environment/SECTORS/Global/Lighting/Baked/Global/DL_Moon_01
  Unity Light: Directional, Baked, intensity 0.5, colour (0.59, 0.63, 1.0)
    (pale blue), soft shadows strength 0.65, bounce 0.5 - component DISABLED.
  BakeryDirectLight (the one that counts):
    colour       (0.588, 0.627, 1.0)   pale blue moonlight
    intensity    0.2
    shadowSpread 0.03                  (soft-ish edge; angular size)
    samples      16
    bakeToIndirect 1, indirectIntensity 0.5 (bounce at half strength)
    shadowmask   0                     (fully baked, not Mixed)
    no cloud shadow texture
  Direction: down 48.5 degrees; it shines from roughly 27 degrees off world
  +Z towards +X (north-north-east if +Z is north).
  RenderSettings.m_Sun is empty.

  So there is NO realtime directional light: the moon exists only in the
  lightmaps and the Bakery Volume. Moving things get moonlight from the
  volume, never a realtime shadow.

  Intensity 0.2 is very low: the moon is a soft blue fill, not a strong key.
  The mood comes from the sky light (below), the colour grade and many warm
  local lights.


5. AMBIENT AND ENVIRONMENT LIGHT
--------------------------------

Bakery Sky Light (baked ambient)
  .../Lighting/Baked/Global/Skylight
    colour (0.588, 0.627, 1.0), same blue as the moon
    intensity 1, samples 32, hemispherical ON (upper half only: no light from
    below the horizon)
    sky texture: a cubemap from an HDRI ("BeautifulCorner ... sky")
    bakeToIndirect 1, indirectIntensity 1
  This is the main source of "you can still see in the dark": the whole level
  gets a baked blue sky bounce, stronger than the moon itself.

Unity ambient (RenderSettings, environment scene)
  Ambient mode Skybox (0), intensity 1. The Trilight colours stored are Unity
  defaults (sky 0.212,0.227,0.259 / equator 0.114,0.125,0.133 / ground
  0.047,0.043,0.035), unused in Skybox mode. Reflection source skybox,
  resolution 128, 1 bounce, intensity 1. Subtractive shadow colour default.
  With Bakery lightmaps and a Bakery Volume on every lit surface and mover,
  Unity's ambient SH barely matters (inferred). The other three scenes carry
  default settings (fog off, default skybox).

Light for movers - Bakery Volume (instead of light probes)
  .../Lighting/BakeryVolumes/GL_BakeryVolume
    global volume (isGlobal 1), bounds centre (8, 6, -33), extents
    (41.5, 10.9, 24.3) = a box 83 x 22 x 48m over the whole level
    1 voxel per metre: 83 x 21 x 48 voxels
    three baked 3D textures (L1 spherical harmonics: direction-aware light)
    plus a shadowmask 3D texture; encoding 0 (uncompressed), no denoise
  Objects tagged BakeryVolumeUser (6 in the environment scene, 63 in the
  gameplay scene: guards, props, pickups) sample it. No LightProbeGroups in
  any scene.

  Material controls on the level shaders (SWG_BakeryURP*VolumeGraph, 115
  materials):
    _Bakery_Volume_Amount: 1 on 101 materials; 0.28-0.5 on a few
    _Bakery_Volume_Clamp_Min_Max: min 0.02 (17), 0.06 (15), 0 (6),
      0.04-0.07 (others), max 1 - a FLOOR on the volume light, so a mover
      never goes fully black
    _Min_Brightness: 0 on 80 materials; 0.01-0.28 on ~22 - a second floor
    _AO_Override_Multiplier mostly 0
  These floors are the per-material "keep it readable" trick: dark areas are
  dark but never pure black on things the player must see.

Reflection probes (all Baked, 128px, HDR, no box projection, blend 1m)
  RP_Global_01           82 x 34 x 66m, importance 1
  RP_RightAlley_01       18 x 11 x 18m, importance 1
  RP_RightAlley_02        8 x 11 x 11m, importance 2
  RP_LeftAlley_01        29 x 11 x 14m, importance 2
  RP_RecruitmentCentre_01 15 x 11 x 14m, importance 2
  Plus one in the gameplay scene's combat tutorial area (18 x 5 x 18m).


6. BAKED LOCAL LIGHTS (BAKERY)
------------------------------

  310 BakeryPointLight components in the environment scene, all enabled
  except 3. Bakery projMode: 0 = omni (point), 4 = cone (spot). Bakery
  "cutoff" = range in metres. Most use realisticFalloff (inverse-square) and
  bake their bounce (bakeToIndirect 1); none use shadowmask (all fully
  baked). Colours (most common first):
    (1.00, 0.88, 0.70)  warm white, 54 groups' worth - the default lamp tone
    (1.00, 0.75, 0.38)  amber
    (0.84, 0.62, 0.54)  dusky pink-orange fill
    (0.76, 0.67, 0.54)  dim warm window glow
    (0.59, 0.63, 1.00), (0.47, 0.50, 0.80), (0.54, 0.92, 0.77),
    (0.43, 0.80, 1.00)  cool blues and teals: moonlight and sewer fills
  (Unity stand-in Lights on lamps are colour (1.00, 0.70, 0.27), 38 of them.)

  By group (Environment/SECTORS/Global/Lighting/...):

  GameplayLights/Prologue_LightingPrefab - the visible lamp fittings, one
  prefab per fixture (wall lamps, ceiling lamps, chandelier, exterior lamps,
  ~39 fittings) and 21 table lamps:
    Cone lamps: TWO Bakery lights each -
      a cone: intensity 5, range 8m, angle 95 deg, soft (shadowSpread 0.5),
        indirectIntensity 0 (no bounce from the cone itself)
      a small omni at the bulb: intensity 1, range 2.5m, shadowSpread 0.05,
        falloffMinRadius 0.3 - lights the fitting and wall right round it
      (one exterior wall lamp: cone 5 / 8m + omni 2 / 5m; one ceiling lamp:
      cone 10 / 12m / 106 deg)
      The Unity stand-in for the cone: Spot, Baked, intensity 5, range 8,
      outer 95 / inner 59 deg, disabled.
    Table lamps: one omni, intensity 1.5, range 3m (one 5m),
      indirectIntensity 0.2.
    Samples 8. This "cone down + tiny omni at the source" pair is the
    standard recipe for every lamp.

  Baked/EnvironmentLights/Fill lights - 102 lights. Designer fill: intensity
    0.3 to 5 (16 different values), range 1.4 to 15m (24 values), mostly
    omni, some cones (45-95 deg), soft (shadowSpread up to 1),
    falloffMinRadius 1-5m (big soft blobs), indirectIntensity 0, 1 or 5.
    These sculpt the darkness: they put light where play needs it rather
    than where a fitting is.
  Baked/EnvironmentLights/Window Lights - 57. Very weak warm glows behind/at
    windows: intensity 0.02 to 1 (typical 0.2), range 1.0 to 3m (typical
    1.3), warm (0.76, 0.67, 0.54). They make lit windows read as lit without
    lighting the street.
  Sewer_MA (15), Sewer_LeftAlley (13), Sewer Lights (4) - sewer fills:
    intensity 0.1 to 3, range 1.5 to 6.3m, some cones, cool tints;
    indirectIntensity up to 5 (bounce pushed hard to fill tunnels).
  Room_Lights (5) / Room Lights (1) - interiors: intensity 0.4 to 12.8,
    range 3.25 to 12m.
  Lamp Lights (6) - street lamps: omni intensity 0.2 to 1, range 1.3 to
    5m, plus a cone at intensity 10, range 10.8m, 112 deg.
  Brazier Lights (3) - intensity 0.5 / 10 / 15, range 4.4 to 10m.
  StartingRoom (1) - a cone at intensity 40, range 9m, 89 deg (a strong
    shaft in the opening room).
  SetDressing searchlight (1) - cone intensity 80, range 25m, 32 deg,
    indirect 0 (Unity stand-in: Spot, intensity 100, range 28.6m).
  MarketAlley (2) - overhead wall lamp cone 10 / 10m / 112 deg + omni.

  Common Bakery settings across all: samples 16 (lamps 8), bitmask 1 (one
  light layer), legacySampling on, shadowSpread 0.05-0.15 for normal lamps.

  Unity Light stand-ins in the environment scene: 46, all disabled (one,
  the recruitment-station fireplace, enabled but on an inactive object).
  Their light-mode is "Baked" for most and "Realtime" for a few, but none

  Scene sectors: BakerySector components split the bake by area (e.g.

7. REALTIME AND MIXED LIGHTS
----------------------------

  Only in the gameplay scene, and only the fires the player can put out
  (all under pfb_PrologueGameplayCore/= Interactables/+ ArrowTargets&Fire):

    Object                                 Light  I     Range  Shadows
    StartingHouse  pfb_CandleGroup (1)     Point  1.25  2m     hard
    StartingHouse  pfb_CandleGroup (2)     Point  1.25  2m     hard
    RecruitmentHall pfb_CandleGrouphouse   Point  1.5   3m     hard
    RecruitmentHall Light_Fireplace        Point  0.5   10.7m  soft 0.75
    market alley   Brazier_pfb (2)         Point  5     5.5m   soft 0.75
    Back Streets   Brazier_pfb (3)         Point  5     4.4m   soft 0.75
  All: Mixed, colour (1.00, 0.54, 0.15) deep orange, bounce 0 (candles,
  fireplace) or 1 (braziers).

  Each also has a BakeryPointLight with bakeToIndirect 0, shadowmask 1,
  indirectIntensity 0 (one at 1): the light's direct part is drawn in
  realtime, its shadows from static geometry come from the baked shadowmask,
  and it leaves NO baked bounce. Put the fire out, disable the light, and the
  area goes dark with no glow left behind.

  HighLevelLight on each: shadowStrength 0 normally, 0.75 at "high level"
  (inferred: a higher graphics tier). So realtime shadows from moving things
  exist only on the higher setting; normally these lights cast no realtime
  shadow at all.

  MazeCustomLight (1-2 per fire): lightingAttenuationType 1,
  lightAttenuationExponent 0.7 (4), 0.9 (3), 1.0 (2) - a custom falloff
  curve (inferred: softer than inverse-square, so a small fire reaches a
  bit further).

  LightFlickerEffect (6): intensityVariance 0, minimumRangeVariance 0,
  flicker frequency 0.1-1 Hz, strength 5, moves the light within +/-5cm
  (movementStrength 0.5) - flicker by moving the light (shadows and
  highlights shimmer) rather than pulsing brightness.

  CandleGroup: candles [3], lightSource, visibilityVolume;
  radiusReductionPerLight 0.5, lightIntensityReductionPerLight 0.2,
  anomalyRadiusReductionPerLight 0.5 - each candle snuffed shrinks the
  group's light and its gameplay light radius. InteractableFire: LightVolume,
  VFX, SFX, objects shown/hidden while lit, cooldown 5s, starts lit.

  Combat tutorial: three Realtime point lights (blue 0.31,0.55,0.80 at 20 and
  40; teal at 5; range 10m; no shadows), inactive in the shipped level.

  Blob shadows: BlobShadowManager/Controller present (max distance 50m) but
  shadowsEnabled 0, maxShadows 0: off in the prologue. A "Simple Lit - Blob
  Shadows Receiver" shader exists.

9. SKYBOX, FOG AND SURROUNDINGS
-------------------------------

Skybox - material SWG_Skybox_Prologue, shader SWG/Skybox_Masked:
  _StaticTex  SkyBox_Prologue cubemap (static sky: moon, stars, horizon)
  _Tex        SkyBox_CloudLayer_BC cubemap (cloud layer)
  _BlendAmount 1
  _Exposure   1.1
  _Rotation   214 deg
  _Tint       (0.118, 0.102, 0.180)  dark violet on the dynamic layer
  _TintStatic white
  _FlowmapParam (0, -0.02, 0.78, 2.27) = opacity 0, strength -0.02, wind
    direction (0.78, 2.27) - clouds drift by flow-map distortion
  The tint makes the cloud layer very dark: the sky reads near-black violet
  with the static sky showing through.
  SpacewarpSky (gameplay scene, Level_Core): a sky sphere renderer for
  application spacewarp (Quest), so the sky gets motion vectors (inferred).

Fog (environment scene RenderSettings):
  on, mode Exponential (2), density 0.015, colour (0.109, 0.104, 0.149)
  dark blue-violet. Linear start/end (0/300) unused.
  What that means: fog fraction = 1 - e^(-0.015 d):
    5m 7%, 10m 14%, 20m 26%, 46m 50%, 100m 78%, 200m 95%.
  The level's far ends sink into blue-violet dark; this also hides lightmap
  detail and pop-in at range. Fog shader variants Exp and Exp2 are kept
  (GraphicsSettings).

Fake god rays - shader Unlit/FakeGodRays (transparent cone/cylinder
  meshes, no lighting): rim fade, vertical fade, distance fade, scrolling
  noise and lines, conify. Uses in the level:
    FakeGodRays_Tavern_Lamp            38 (under lamp fittings)
    FakeGodRays_Security_SpotLight_02   1 (the searchlight)
    ...GodRays_NobleManorWindows        5, PrologueWindows 2 + 2 orange
  Security spotlight example: base colour (0.22, 0.24, 0.27), final alpha
  0.2, rim power 1.45, distance fade 8m start / 14m length, far fade from
  40m. All placed geometry: no screen-space or volumetric effect.

Light flares - corona billboards (shader Billboard/CoronaBillboard):
  Corona_Flare 60, Corona_Smalllamp 19, Corona_Flare_Candle 9,
  Corona_Flare_Brazier 2, Corona_SecuritySpot 1. Driven by
  LightVolumeFlareVFX with LightFlareData assets, e.g.
  LightFlareDataInteriorCovered: transparency 0 to max 0.2, eased by
  distance (peaks at 60% of its range), fades in over 1s, out over 2s, tint
  (1.0, 0.98, 0.75). The flare stands in for bloom: bright lamps glow
  without a post bloom pass.
  Also emissive "glow" materials: fx_flies_glow 43 (fireflies/moths round
  lamps), fx_CandleFlame_Glow 9, glow 5.


10. GAMEPLAY LIGHT (STEALTH) - HOW "LIT" IS DECIDED
--------------------------------------------------

  Not from the rendered lighting. Each gameplay light carries a hand-sized
  trigger volume:
    LightVisibilityVolume (sphere) - 19 in the environment scene, 6 in
      gameplay; radii 2m (19), 3.4m (26), 5.5m (15), 6.6m, plus 2.71,
      2.93, 4.37 for fires
    LightVisibilityVolumeCone - 42 (the cone lamps)
    each with lightOrigin, isStatic, IsDarkVolume (a volume can mark dark),
    a light intensity tween and links to its flare and flicker.
  LightVisibilityEntity on characters lists body nodes (with
  isPlayerNode / requiresPlayerToBeGrounded) checked against the volumes;
  LightVisibilityManager runs it. LightVolumeSaveable (61 + 6) remembers
  which lights are on. Extinguishing a fire turns its volume off; snuffing
  candles shrinks the radius (CandleGroup above).


TAKEAWAYS FOR TEALEAF
---------------------

- Their brightness/gamma needs a post pass (URP uber post + LUT) every
  frame. Under TeaLeaf's rule (never depend on a full-screen effect; an
  optional, switchable one is allowed once measured) the same two-slider
  design fits only as an optional setting that turns post on while either
  slider is away from 0. A no-post route would be global shader values read
  in TeaLeaf's own level shaders (the way their GlobalColorGradingController
  grades), but that misses pack URP Lit materials.
- Their gameplay readability comes from baked layers, not post: a strong
  blue hemispherical sky light, a weak blue moon (0.2), the shadows x1.8
  blue grade, light-volume floors (_Bakery_Volume_Clamp min 0.02-0.06,
  _Min_Brightness) and fog colour matched to the darkness.
- The extinguishable fire set-up matches TeaLeaf's plan: Mixed, shadowmask,
  no baked bounce (TeaLeaf's Indirect Multiplier 0); realtime shadow
  strength only on a higher quality setting.
- Lamp recipe worth copying: a downward cone (I 5, 8m, 95 deg) plus a tiny
