THIEF VR vs TEALEAF - GUARD DETECTION
=====================================

Source: the extracted Thief VR prologue (E:\ThiefVR_Export\Prologue\ExportedProject).
The game is IL2CPP, so the export has no code: every script is an empty stub.
Everything here comes from serialized data - the guard data asset
(Guard_Data_Default), components on guards and the player rig, prefabs,
and Behavior Designer task names. The structure and numbers are real; how
the pieces combine at runtime is inferred and marked so where unsure.


SIDE BY SIDE
------------

Meters
  Thief VR: two per guard, SUSPICION and DETECTION. Suspicion starts draining
    1s after the last stimulus, at 1/s. Detection holds 4s, then drains at
    0.25/s.
  TeaLeaf:  the same two meters, holds and rates; suspicion full = Suspicious,
    detection full = chase, detection 0.5 = worth a search.

Vision shape
  Thief VR: four convex mesh trigger colliders 1.8m up on the body, facing
    the body's way. Physics does the shape test, not code.
  TeaLeaf:  one view frustum on the eye (Peripheral) split into the same
    four zones by rules in code (GuardSight): each an angle and a cap
    across, up and down, plus a narrowing towards its far end. Fitted to
    the meshes; Very Close and Peripheral come out a little bigger.
    Linecasts only for points a zone can see.

Vision zones (Thief VR mesh vertices; x half width, y relative to origin)
  Very close  - 3m deep, +/-1.5m wide, 2.1m below to 0.95m above.
  Close       - +/-0.5m at the origin, +/-4.5m at 1.5m (about 144 deg),
                +/-4.75m at 3.7m, narrowing to +/-2.1m at 6m.
  Direct LOS  - +/-1m at 0.5m to +/-5m at 9m (about 58 deg), then a 10m-wide
                corridor to 30m; 4m below to 2m above.
  Peripheral  - +/-7.25m at 1.7m (about 154 deg), +/-12.5m at 20-30m,
                reaching 6.8m below eye level.
  TeaLeaf:  the same, fitted as angles and caps (above).

How fast it fills
  Thief VR: a table per zone and distance band. Each band has a fill time for
    suspicion and one for detection, a lit set and a dark set, and multipliers
    for standing still, crouching and moving fast (above a speed threshold of
    1.4-3 m/s).
  TeaLeaf:  the same table and numbers. Fastest zone wins, times blended
    across a band, a multiplier of 0 = never (all three inferred).

Seen in the dark
  Thief VR: within 1.5m detected in 0.2s; 1.5-3m in 0.5s; 3-6m suspicion over
    3-6s. Beyond 6m straight ahead, or in peripheral vision at any distance:
    impossible (dark fill time -1). Moving fast in the dark close up cuts the
    fill time to x0.02-0.05, so it is almost instant.
  TeaLeaf:  the same; dark = Visibility below 0.3, which includes crouching
    (crouched in moonlight counts as dark).

Points tested on the player
  Thief VR: four points round the head plus the waist (raised 0.15m when
    crouched), weighted: head sides and top 0.17 each, below the head 0.5,
    body 0.8. A guard needs a total weight of at least 0.5, so in practice the
    body, or the head from below plus something else, must show.
  TeaLeaf:  the same five points and weights (PlayerVisibility).

Light
  Thief VR: hand-placed light volumes - 67 in the prologue (25 spheres of
    radius 2-6.6m, 42 cones mostly 40 deg). The player has two light points,
    one at the head and one at the feet that only counts when grounded. Each
    point is lit or dark, nothing between, and that picks the lit or dark
    column of the table.
  TeaLeaf:  from geometry - one ray to the moon plus LightSource rays;
    continuous 0-1, brightest light wins; no volumes.

Touch
  Thief VR: touched by a hand - suspicion in 0.01s, detection in 0.001s,
    effectively instant detection.
  TeaLeaf:  a bump fills suspicion and takes detection to the investigate
    level, guard becomes Suspicious, no chase.

Hearing
  Thief VR: five fixed noise levels - silent, low, medium, high, very high -
    radii 0 / 2 / 5 / 10 / 15m. Range also checked as a NavMesh path distance
    (1.25x the radius). Each floor has a loudness: carpet 1, most floors 2,
    glass and loose wood 3. Footsteps count as stealthy up to 1.9 m/s.
  TeaLeaf:  continuous loudness, routed through sound rooms and portals, added
    to the meter x0.7 (x1.5 for the noisemaker); footsteps vary by surface and
    stance.

Player's real voice
  Thief VR: microphone above a 0.12 threshold counts as a shout that alerts
    guards (2.5s cooldown).
  TeaLeaf:  no.

Things guards notice ("anomalies")
  Thief VR: torches put out (with an investigation priority; guards walk over
    and relight torches and braziers), knocked-out, dead and injured guards.
    The far sight bands see these only if lit.
  TeaLeaf:  knocked-out guards in light >= 0.15 only. A fire going out is on
    the not-built list.

Guard to guard
  Thief VR: barks carry 3m nearby, 15m shouted. Guards alert others within
    10m (also a 10m path) without needing sound. A guard turns hostile on
    seeing another die within 9m.
  TeaLeaf:  none (not built).

After losing the player
  Thief VR: re-acquires instantly if it sees the player again within 1.5s and
    20m. Alert lasts 9s, the hunt times out after 30s, an investigation lasts
    5s.
  TeaLeaf:  chaseLostTime 4s, then a search.

Search
  Thief VR: first search spot within 40m of the target, 20s / 3m cooldown per
    spot. Later spots 8-15m from the target and at least 4.5m from the guard,
    45s cooldown. Five 1.5m rays pick which look-around animation to play, so
    a guard never searches into a wall.
  TeaLeaf:  three random NavMesh points within 4m; look-about turns a random
    60-120 deg.

Logic
  Thief VR: Behavior Designer trees (Patrol, Suspicious, ...).
  TeaLeaf:  switch state machine.

Speeds
  Thief VR: patrol 2 m/s, alert 4 m/s.
  TeaLeaf:  patrol 1.07, chase 3.6.

Other
  Thief VR: awareness meter shown above each guard's head; light gem with
    haptics; volumes that stop the player peeking through walls.
  TeaLeaf:  light gem on the compass; no meter shown in game.


WHAT IT ADDS UP TO
------------------

- Sight is now theirs in TeaLeaf: their zones, bands, lit/dark fill times,
  multipliers, five weighted points and two meters. It gives their sharp,
  readable rules ("never seen in the dark past 6m, never in peripheral
  vision unless lit") at the cost of a large tuning table (GuardSight).
- Their light is designer volumes; ours comes from geometry. TeaLeaf rules
  out volumes (no designer-placed shadow/light volumes). Our continuous 0-1
  Visibility is cut at a lit threshold to pick their lit or dark column.
- Movement matters much more than before: in the dark the moving-fast
  multiplier (x0.02) is the main thing that gives the player away up close.


IDEAS WORTH TAKING
------------------

None break the visibility-from-geometry rule. Each is a design choice; nothing
built.

1. Instant re-acquire after losing the player briefly (their 1.5s / 20m).
   Stops the guard looking dim when the player ducks behind a pillar.
2. Guards noticing a torch going out, then investigating and relighting it.
   Already on the not-built list; Flame is the natural hook.
3. Short rays before a look-about turn so a searching guard doesn't stare at
   a wall. About five rays per search stop.
4. Guard-to-guard alerts. On the roadmap as not built; theirs use a 10m path
   range plus bark ranges.

Leave out: the microphone (out of scope, hardware dependency), hand-placed
light volumes (against the design rule).

Not yet examined: the Suspicious behaviour tree in detail and the
ChangeAnimatorAwarenessLevel animator states.
