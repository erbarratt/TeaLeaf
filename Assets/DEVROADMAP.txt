TeaLeaf Development Roadmap - Vertical Slice
============================================

Goal: a single greybox level (warehouse/docks) that runs in Play Mode and
showcases every core stealth mechanic end to end - sneak in, steal the
objective item, escape - with a fail/restart loop when caught. No settings
menu, main menu, save system or final art required.

[x] = done, [ ] = to do. Done items are kept as one line; how each works
is in the CLAUDE.md files, and how it got there is in DEVLOG.txt.


Vertical slice feature list
---------------------------
Movement:     walk, smooth/snap turn, gravity, crouch, sprint, jump, ledge
              climbing, mantling, ladders, rope climbing, zip lines.
Visibility:   worked out from the level's geometry - moonlight, shadowed
              wherever something stands between the player and the moon,
              plus torches and lamps within their range. Extinguishing a
              torch darkens what it lit. Wrist-mounted visibility gem for
              feedback.
Noise:        footsteps by surface type (scaled by sprint/walk/crouch),
              thrown/dropped physics objects, doors and other interactions.
Sound:        rooms and portals, so a sound is heard from where it really
              comes; reverb from the room the player is in; looping sounds
              and room ambience; the project's own spatialiser.
Guard AI:     patrol, suspicion, search, chase, catch (= fail + restart).
              Knockout state. Guards react to finding KO'd bodies.
Weapons:      blackjack takedown; a clockwork hand crossbow worn on the back
              of one hand, with water / noisemaker / rope bolts.
Tools:        two-handed lockpicking on a large copy of the lock (one pick
              turns in stages, the other searches for each pin by feel).
              The picks are worn on the back of the other hand.
Inventory:    a pack that only holds so much, brought out by gripping over
              a shoulder; loot put in by hand, one space each; gold, with
              its number; the objective; coloured keys on a keyring.
Controls:     left stick moves, its click toggles sprint; right stick
              turns, up mantles, down toggles crouch; A jumps; B cancels;
              one setting swaps the hands for a left-handed player.
Bodies:       grab and drag KO'd guards to hide them.
Throwing:     aimed (hold the trigger for an arc, release to throw, B to
              cancel) and physical (move the hand and let go of grip).
Hands:        physical hands - the visual hand model collides with the world
              and stops at walls/props instead of passing through, then
              catches back up to the real controller position.
Art:          greybox (ProBuilder/primitives) for environment and props.
              Placeholder humanoid + Mixamo animations for guards. Real hand
              models with finger animation/grip poses.


Performance rules for everything built
--------------------------------------
The target: PCVR, 90 fps with 25% headroom (8.3 ms a frame for the CPU and
for the GPU) at about 2000 x 2000 pixels per eye on an RTX 3060 12 GB, a
Ryzen 5 5600X class processor and 16 GB of RAM - "3060 upwards". Quest 3
standalone is only a possible later port. The cheap time to meet the
target is while each thing is built, not in Phase 10. Every phase follows
these; the fuller form is in the root CLAUDE.md ("Performance rules").
- Nothing has been measured on a 3060-class machine yet. Until it has,
  a cost is a judgement.
- Pixels are the biggest cost (8 million a frame, 90 times a second): the
  game must not depend on a full-screen effect or on the camera depth or
  opaque texture (an optional one that can be switched off is allowed
  once measured), no parallax occlusion on surfaces that fill the view,
  as little see-through overdraw as possible. The procedural cobblestone
  and brick shaders are acceptable, subject to measuring. 4x MSAA is
  wanted.
- Light is baked where it can be; realtime lights are few and
  short-ranged. Torches may cast realtime shadows from moving things,
  with the level's shadows baked, as a quality setting that can be
  turned off.
- Level geometry that never moves is marked Static and shares a small
  number of materials, so it batches, bakes and occlusion-culls.
- Depth the player gets close to is modelled; normal maps are for fine
  detail only.
- Level layout does the culling: bent approaches and doors break long
  sightlines, backed by an occlusion bake.
- Anything costly on the CPU (AI senses, pathfinding, light sampling,
  sound paths) runs a few times a second, staggered, never every frame -
  and less often again when far away or unseen.
- Nothing is created or destroyed during play: pool it.
- Tricks kept in reserve, to try only if profiling asks: light distance
  haze with nearer cull distances for small props, impostors.

Phase 6 - Guard AI (AI namespace)
---------------------------------
Setup:
[ ] Confirm the AI Navigation package; NavMeshSurface on the level.
[ ] Guard prefab: placeholder humanoid, NavMeshAgent, Animator, head
    collider (GuardHead layer), eye transform, capsule body collider.
Perception:
[ ] Vision: cone angle + range, checked on a staggered timer (e.g. 5-10Hz,
    offset per guard), LOS raycasts to 2-3 points on the player (head,
    chest, feet). Detection rate scales with the player's Visibility
    (PlayerVisibility, which already includes light and stance), distance
    and movement. In full shadow the player is only seen at very close
    range.
[ ] Detection meter per guard (0-1) that rises/decays; thresholds drive
    state transitions.
[ ] Hearing: register with NoiseSystem as an INoiseListener. It already
    delivers only the noises that reach the guard through rooms and
    portals, with a loudness and the point it was heard from; louder
    noise = more suspicion.
[ ] Body discovery: seeing a KO'd guard in a lit area jumps to Search at the
    body and raises the whole level's alert level (for the slice: those
    guards search longer).
Behaviour (explicit state machine - keep it readable and debuggable):
[ ] Patrol: waypoint routes (PatrolRoute component with ordered points +
    optional wait/look-around time at each).
[ ] Suspicious: stop, turn towards the stimulus, "huh?" bark; escalate or
    return to patrol.
[ ] Search/Investigate: walk to the last known position, then pick a few
    nearby NavMesh points to check, with a timeout before giving up.
[ ] Chase: run to the player's live position while seen, last known when
    lost; drop back to Search when lost for N seconds.
[ ] Catch: within catch range while chasing, call LevelManager.Caught().
[ ] Knocked out: disable perception/agent; the body becomes draggable.
    KO'd guards stay down for the whole slice.
[ ] Return to patrol after search, resuming the nearest waypoint.
Body dragging:
[ ] KO'd guard has a grab handle (collar/shoulder). The player grabs it and
    the body follows the hand along the ground (kinematic follow with
    ground snapping, or ragdoll with a joint to the hand - start with
    kinematic, it's cheaper and more predictable). Dragging slows the
    player and makes some noise on loud surfaces.
Audio feedback:
[ ] Placeholder barks per state transition (suspicious, searching, gave up,
    spotted, found body) - these are how the player reads AI state.
[ ] Voice lines that don't repeat. Guards draw from very large pools of
    lines - big enough that a usual playthrough of a level never hears
    the same line twice - and a line isn't played again until every
    other line in its pool has been used. Barks use this too, and so
    does idle chatter: now and then a guard on a normal patrol says
    something to himself. Several guard voices: each voice has its own
    set of pools (one per kind of line), each guard is given a voice and
    keeps it, and "used" is tracked per voice's pool. Other guards don't
    react to idle chatter - a spoken line is sound only, not a noise
    event.
    [ ] Voice line pool (Core, beside SoundCue, which picks a clip at
        random and so can repeat): one pool per kind of line (idle
        mutter, suspicious, searching, gave up, spotted, found body),
        each a long list of clips. Lightweight: a shuffled order of clip
        numbers and a place in it - take the next one each time; when
        the list runs out, shuffle again, making sure the new first line
        isn't the one just played. Built once, nothing allocated per
        line.
    [ ] Shared and lasting: one record per pool for all guards with that
        voice, kept for the whole play session - a level restart must
        not start the lines over. So it lives outside the scene, and
        isn't saved to disk.
    [ ] Played through SoundPlayer like any other sound (rooms and
        portals, muffling), from the guard's head. One line at a time
        per guard; a state bark cuts off an idle mutter.
    [ ] Idle chatter: on patrol only, at a random interval with a long
        minimum gap, and not while another guard nearby is talking.
    [ ] Later, maybe: conversations between two guards. Keep the pool
        usable for it: a conversation would be one entry that isn't
        repeated, the same as a single line.
    [ ] Placeholder lines to prove the system; the large pools are a
        recording job - list the lines wanted per pool, and how many.
Debug (Scripts/AI/Debug):
[ ] Gizmos: vision cones, hearing radius, patrol routes, last-known
    position, current state label and detection meter above each guard.
Performance:
[ ] Budget: ~4-6 guards in the slice. Stagger perception, avoid
    allocations in state updates, and animator culling mode = cull
    completely when offscreen.
[ ] Guards far from the player or out of sight think less often: a
    slower perception timer by distance, and no vision rays at all for a
    guard too far away to see the player. Path requests spread out,
    never several in one frame.
[ ] One skinned mesh and one material per guard, a modest bone count.
    Guards cast shadows from torches through the torch shadow setting
    (Phase 8).

Phase 8 - Level: the docks warehouse
------------------------------------
[x] The smithy: a building made from code as level buildings could be
    (TeaLeaf > Build Smithy).
Design (on paper first):
[ ] Layout sketch with a critical path and at least one alternate route per
    area. Each beat below should teach or test one mechanic, roughly in
    this order:
    1. Start: dockside at night, dark. Crouch and move quietly (wood dock
       vs metal ramp teaches surface noise).
    2. Yard: one patrolling guard, crates, shadows between the lamps.
       Teaches reading light/dark and patrol timing.
    3. Torch-lit gate: extinguish a torch with a water bolt to cross.
    4. Climb: ledge climb + mantle onto crates, a ladder up to a
       catwalk/roof.
    5. Rope bolt: a wooden beam high on the warehouse wall gives an
       alternate rooftop route.
    6. Warehouse interior: two guards on crossing patrols, catwalks
       overhead, loud metal floor below. Noisemaker bolt / thrown bottle to
       pull a guard off a doorway.
    7. Takedown: a lone guard facing away in a dead end - blackjack, then
       drag the body into shadow (body-discovery teaches the hiding
       reason).
    8. Office: locked door (lockpick) and a locked chest/safe with the
       objective item. More loot scattered around than the pack holds.
    9. Escape: back out to a boat/exit zone, a guard now searching near
       the route in.
[ ] Loot and bolt counts: enough bolts to solve each beat with a small
    spare; tune after playtesting.
[ ] Lay it out so the level culls itself: no long straight views across
    the whole site - bent approaches, the yard wall, the warehouse's own
    walls and doors between areas, so that from any spot only one or two
    areas can be seen. The interior and the docks should rarely be on
    screen together.
Greybox build:
[ ] Block out in ProBuilder at true VR scale (door heights 2.1m, ceilings,
    catwalk rail heights, ledge heights tested at 1.2-2m).
[ ] Mark everything that never moves Static (nothing in the scene is but
    the smithy and the door test wall, and the other test-area builders
    don't set it), on the Environment layer, with a small number of
    shared materials - one material and one colour-swatch texture can
    cover the greybox. Box/capsule colliders where they fit; mesh
    colliders only where the shape needs one.
[ ] Small props (bottles, coins, crates) are on Interactable, not
    Environment, so that layer can be given a nearer cull distance than
    walls if profiling asks for it.
[ ] Tag surfaces (SurfaceType), RopeTarget beams, ClimbableEdges, ladders.
[ ] Place the sound rooms and portals, with each room's reverb and
    ambience, and a SoundLoop on each torch and fire.
[ ] Place the LightSources, each with its visible Unity light.
[ ] Bake the NavMesh; add NavMesh links/obstacles for doors; verify guards
    can't path onto player-only routes (catwalks/roofs).
[ ] Place guards + PatrolRoutes, loot, bolt pickups, objective, exit zone,
    player start.
Lighting:
[ ] Baked lighting with light probes for the static environment; torches
    as a small number of mixed lights (baked lights can't be turned off
    by a water bolt). Keep realtime lights per view low and ranges
    short.
    [ ] The moon. It never moves, so its light and shadows on the level
        can be baked. To try first: the Directional Light as Mixed,
        lighting mode Shadowmask - the level's shadows come from the
        lightmap, and only moving things (guards, props, hands) are
        drawn into a small, short-distance shadow map. Fully realtime,
        as now, redraws the whole level into the shadow map each frame.
    [ ] Torches. A realtime point light with no shadows shines through
        walls, while the gameplay light (LightSource) is blocked by
        them. The plan (untested): Mixed lights in Shadowmask mode with
        indirect light at zero - the light itself stays realtime, so a
        water bolt can put it out and no baked glow is left behind, and
        its shadows from the level are baked, so walls block it for no
        shadow map. On top, realtime shadows from moving things only
        (guards, props, hands), as a quality setting that can be turned
        off. A point light's shadow map is six views: if that costs too
        much, try spot lights for wall torches (one view) or shadows on
        the nearest one or two torches only. At most four mixed lights
        may overlap at any one spot (the moon counts as one if Mixed).
    [ ] Keep the lights reaching any one object few (URP Forward drops
        the extra ones, and they pop). Keep torch ranges short and don't
        merge the level into a few huge meshes - or test Forward+.
    [ ] Light probes along every route the player, guards and props
        use; one baked reflection probe, or none.
    [ ] A Meta pass for the cobblestone and brick shaders, so a lightmap
        bake can see their colours.
[ ] Make sure visible light matches the gameplay light (SceneLight) -
    the player must be able to trust what they see. Every mesh that
    casts a shadow has a collider on Environment or Interactable.
[ ] Occlusion culling bake; split the level into chunks that cull cleanly.
[ ] Set the camera's far clip plane to a little more than the longest
    view in the level (the sky doesn't depend on it; it is 200m now).
[ ] If profiling asks for it: light distance haze (URP's built-in fog,
    not a depth-based effect) with nearer cull distances for small
    props. It needs the sky to fade into the same haze at the horizon,
    and must stay thin enough to read lit and dark areas from afar.
Audio:
[ ] Ambient beds (water lapping, distant bells, warehouse hum), footsteps
    per surface, door creaks, impacts, crossbow cock/fire, bolt effects,
    lockpick clicks, loot pickup, guard barks, alert/chase stinger.

Phase 10 - Performance pass (PCVR target rig)
---------------------------------------------
[ ] Find a way to measure against the target rig (RTX 3060 12 GB, Ryzen
    5 5600X class, 16 GB): a machine of that class, or a tested scaling
    from the dev machine.
[ ] Baseline: frame times for the CPU and the GPU at about 2000 x 2000
    per eye and 90Hz, with the Unity Profiler and SteamVR's frame
    timing, streamed through Virtual Desktop as the dev headset is (the
    video encode takes some of the GPU's time). Target: 8.3 ms each -
    90 fps with 25% headroom.
[ ] Rendering: draw call/batch count, SRP Batcher compatibility, MSAA,
    shadows, render scale. To settle on the target rig (PC_RPAsset is
    what a PC build uses):
    [x] PC render settings trimmed: no ambient occlusion, no depth or
        opaque texture, HDR off, two shadow cascades with hard edges.
    [ ] Switch off the features nothing uses (additional-light shadows
        until torches need them, light cookies, reflection probe
        blending) and choose between Forward and Forward+.
    [ ] 4x MSAA at render scale 1.0; come down only if the frame time
        says so.
    [ ] Dynamic resolution as the safety net for heavy moments.
    [ ] Environment and prop materials are URP Lit (full PBR). Compare
        Simple Lit and Baked Lit once lighting is baked.
    [ ] The procedural surface shaders (TeaLeaf/Cobblestone and
        TeaLeaf/BrickWall) work their pattern out per pixel, on surfaces
        that fill the view. Profile them, with and without Dirt and
        Parallax; the cobbles' 5x5 search is the heavy one. If they cost
        too much, bake each pattern into textures at level load (as the

Milestones
----------
M1  Movement complete        Phases 0-1  (all traversal in a test area)
M2  Stealth core             Phase 2     (visibility, noise, fail/win state)
M3  Hands on everything      Phases 3-5  (grab, inventory, all tools)

                                         target rig)

Sound Test House) so each system is proven in isolation before it goes
into the level.
